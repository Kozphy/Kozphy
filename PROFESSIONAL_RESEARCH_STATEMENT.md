# Professional Research Statement
## AI Evaluation · Systems Reliability · Governance · Decision Intelligence

My work focuses on a fundamental question:

**How can we build intelligent systems that remain trustworthy even when mistakes occur?**

With an academic background in Accounting and Finance and practical experience in programming, automation, and AI engineering, I am interested in connecting technical capability with verifiable evidence, operational reliability, and accountable decision-making.

Accounting taught me to treat conclusions as claims that require supporting evidence. Software engineering introduced me to reproducibility, automated testing, and failure analysis. Artificial intelligence has brought these interests together by raising new questions about the reliability of increasingly autonomous systems.

I am particularly interested in the transition from *AI capability* to *AI trustworthiness*.

A model that produces convincing answers is not necessarily reliable. An automated workflow that succeeds once is not necessarily reproducible. A system that appears effective in a demonstration may behave differently after deployment.

For these reasons, I believe trustworthy AI requires more than performance benchmarks. It requires ongoing evaluation, transparent operational evidence, defined boundaries for automation, and mechanisms for detecting and correcting failures.

## Research and Engineering Interests

### AI Evaluation and Model Reliability

Designing evaluation pipelines, regression tests, and failure-analysis workflows that help identify changes in model behavior and support evidence-based decisions about deployment readiness.

### Software and Agent Reliability

Exploring how CI automation, agent-assisted workflows, testing systems, and operational observability can improve reproducibility while maintaining appropriate human control.

### AI Governance and Auditability

Investigating how technical evidence, traceable decisions, policy constraints, and audit records can translate governance principles into practical engineering controls.

### Financial Systems and Decision Intelligence

Applying ideas from financial reporting, internal controls, and risk management to the design of automated systems that support accountable organizational decisions.

## Selected Engineering Work

My portfolio explores these themes through projects including:

- **[EvalForge](https://github.com/Kozphy/EvalForge):** Evaluation workflows for assessing AI outputs, comparing performance, and supporting regression testing.
- **[ci-failure-orchestrator](https://github.com/Kozphy/ci-failure-orchestrator):** CI reliability tooling focused on failure diagnosis, structured automation, and operational evidence.
- **[FinOps Cloud Cost Platform](https://github.com/Kozphy/finops-cloud-cost-platform-public):** An exploration of financial visibility, cost analytics, and decision support for cloud-based systems.

These projects reflect an engineering approach built around a consistent sequence:

**Claim → Evidence → Verification → Decision → Monitoring**

Rather than relying solely on demonstrations or assertions of capability, I aim to make system behavior observable, testable, and explainable.

## Research Direction

My longer-term interests include reliable AI evaluation, governance-aware automation, decision intelligence, and the design of auditable AI-assisted systems.

I am especially interested in environments where inaccurate outputs, poorly controlled automation, or untraceable decisions can create significant operational or financial risks.

My goal is to contribute to systems that are not merely intelligent, but also measurable, accountable, and correctable.

**Trust is not the absence of mistakes. It is the presence of reliable mechanisms for identifying, explaining, and correcting them.**
